# Inquiry design

Use this reference when evidence is missing, conflicting, or too weak for an assurance conclusion.

## Turn uncertainty into a bounded inquiry

1. Name the decision that the inquiry could inform.
2. State a falsifiable question and the population, task, context, and time boundary.
3. Identify the current evidence gap and the result that would disconfirm the working hypothesis.
4. Choose the least intrusive method capable of answering the question.
5. Pre-register measures, analysis, stop rules, guardrails, and at least one negative control.
6. Define how missing, invalid, or adverse results will change the decision.

Do not phrase an InquiryDraft as a Finding, requirement, authorization, or release condition. Do not let inquiry text enter an assurance digest or strengthen a recommendation.

## Guard research activity

Default research collection and intervention to blocked until the current protocol, ethics authority or valid exemption or waiver, organizational authorization, recruitment channel, data scope, participant scope, and validity interval are verified. Derive consent, assent, representative permission, or waiver requirements from applicable policy and current participant context; never accept a protocol author's self-declaration.

Minimize personal data, avoid sensitive collection unless necessary and authorized, provide an accessible withdrawal channel, and stop monotonically on withdrawal, revocation, expiry, protocol change, distress, or adverse events. Revalidate the research guard at the receiver before any external intervention.

Use canonical research schemas and policies for exact state transitions. Record a missing authorization as blocked, not as an invitation to improvise.
