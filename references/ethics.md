# Ethics

Use this reference as a guardrail for product analysis, design changes, verification, and research.

## Protect people and agency

1. Identify who benefits, who bears cost, and who may be excluded.
2. Preserve informed choice, meaningful refusal, recovery, and accessible alternatives.
3. Minimize requested data, retention, exposure, and secondary use.
4. Avoid manipulation, coercive defaults, deceptive urgency, hidden costs, and obstruction.
5. Consider accessibility across perception, operation, understanding, and robust assistive-technology use.
6. Escalate credible rights, safety, discrimination, privacy, or vulnerability concerns.

Do not claim legal compliance, medical safety, ethical approval, or accessibility conformance from a UX review. Do not treat a specific jurisdiction's practice as a universal obligation. Bind normative statements to verified applicable authority and exact scope.

## Handle vulnerable and research contexts

Use additional care for minors, people under dependency or power imbalance, sensitive inferences, and high-impact eligibility or pricing decisions. Require valid research authorization and participant permissions before collection or intervention. Honor withdrawal immediately and keep already collected data subject to the approved retention decision.

## Keep uncertainty visible

Separate observed facts, assessed claims, hypotheses, and value judgments. Name evidence gaps and affected parties. When evidence or authority is unknown, preserve the uncertainty and choose the registered escalation or blocking behavior.

Read canonical policies and source assertions for exact authority. This reference guides conduct and does not create new policy.
