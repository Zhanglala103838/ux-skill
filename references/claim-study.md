# Claims and studies

Use this reference to keep conclusions no stronger than their evidence.

## Build an assessable claim

1. State one proposition with an explicit subject, relation, value, context, and time scope.
2. Select the claim kind that matches the proposition: normative, causal, associational, predictive, descriptive, reported experience, or observed signal.
3. Bind the proposition to source material or study artifacts that directly support it.
4. Apply the registered assessment policy and preserve every required check.
5. Downgrade or leave unresolved when the stronger claim kind is not supported.

Do not infer causation from sequence, correlation, expert opinion, analytics, or a before-and-after observation without an adequate identification design. Do not treat a vendor statement or design-system feature as an outcome. Do not use a hypothesis as evidence.

## Assess study evidence

Check protocol identity, population, sampling, assignment, comparator, measurement, missingness, analysis execution, dependency clusters, adverse events, and transport limits. Separate what was planned from what was executed. Treat repeated artifacts from one dependency cluster as one source of support.

Grade validity, directness, precision, and transportability independently. Apply policy caps for contradiction, selection, drift, and incomplete execution. Report the limiting dimension and the evidence boundary.

## Preserve the authority boundary

Only an assessed Claim can support a Finding or Recommendation. An InquiryDraft remains non-authoritative regardless of confident wording. Brand reputation, visual similarity, popularity, and third-party awards do not raise an evidence grade.

Read exact source propositions and assessment rules from the canonical knowledge files. Keep prose summaries subordinate to those artifacts.
